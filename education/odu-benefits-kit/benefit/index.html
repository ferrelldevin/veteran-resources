A · How to find out which one you are on

NO NETWORK?
If a linked page will not open: your letter, the SCO, or the VA Education Call Center — 888-442-4551 (TTY 711), also printed in section E. Do not guess a chapter from this screen alone.
why, and what still works offline
This screen carries the VA Education Call Center number and ODU's office phone and email (section E), read from the institutions' own pages on 2026-09-02; it carries no office address, and every route below that says "sign in" or "find" still needs the linked page. If the links will not open, chapter identification from this screen is unavailable. The test that counts is the link: if a linked page will not open, treat this screen as offline for that route. What still works without a network: the LETTER route (your COE or award letter names the chapter); the desk's SCO, who can read your current benefit type in Enrollment Manager — VA: "The remaining entitlement for a student's current benefit type displays in months and days on the student's Profile and Benefits tab" (VA Education Office Hours, Enrollment Manager quick tips, updated 2025-06-04); and a printed copy of this sheet, which carries the numbers on its contact line in section E. Do not guess a chapter from this screen alone.
LETTER
Your Certificate of Eligibility (COE) or award letter names the chapter and, for the GI Bill chapters, your months of entitlement. VA issues one when it approves a claim; VR&E students have a counselor's plan and authorization instead.
ONLINE
Post-9/11 GI Bill only: va.gov/education/check-post-9-11-gi-bill-benefits shows "how much of your benefits you've used and how much you have remaining." The letters tool is the same scope — VA: "Right now, you can use this tool only to get your decision letter for Post-9/11 GI Bill benefits" (va.gov, 2024-11-29).
PHONE
GI Bill chapters (33, 30, 1606, 35, Fry): VA Education Call Center (TTY 711), "Monday through Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 7:00 p.m. ET" (read 2026-09-02) — 888-442-4551 (TTY 711), quoted from the va.gov payments FAQ, updated 2026-08-05; the same number is on the contact line in section E.
CHAPTER 31
VR&E questions do not go to the GI Bill call center: they go to your assigned counselor (VRC). VA's handbook for schools, for a student who cannot reach the counselor: "call or visit your VA Regional Office and ask to speak to the VR&E Supervisor. Someone will assist you in your VRC's absence." (VR&E SCO Handbook § M, updated 2025-07-30). Find the regional office at va.gov/find-locations. If you do not know whether you have a Chapter 31 case at all, that same office can tell you.
ONE TELL
If a VR&E counselor (VRC) approved a rehabilitation plan for your degree and the school receives a training authorization for you, you are on VR&E (Chapter 31). A VetSuccess on Campus counselor or VA career counseling (Chapter 36) does not make you Chapter 31. The clearance form you file with the school does not tell you which GI Bill chapter you are on — your COE does; if you have none, the call center can tell you, and the certifying office can tell you what has been certified for you at this school (a Chapter 31 authorization reaches it from your counselor, not from you).
ANOTHER ONE?
Could you qualify for a benefit you are not using?
the four screens, in VA's terms
Four screens, each in VA's terms, each ending in the form to file: a service-connected rating of 10% or more (or the active-duty routes on VA's eligibility page) → you may apply for VR&E, and a VRC decides entitlement — with a VA rating on VA Form 28-1900; a service member without a rating yet (PEB, IDES, or a 20% memorandum rating) on VA Form 28-0588, "Getting Ahead After You Get Out" (same page, updated 2026-04-09); active duty after 9/10/2001 — 90 days or more for a tier, "36+ months" for 100%, or a Purple Heart since 9/11, or "30+ continuous days" followed by a service-connected disability discharge (va.gov rates page) → Post-9/11 GI Bill, VA Form 22-1990; you paid into the Montgomery GI Bill on active duty, or you have a six-year Selected Reserve obligation → Chapter 30 or 1606, VA Form 22-1990; you are the child or spouse of a Veteran or service member "who has died, is captured or missing, or is permanently and totally disabled due to a service-connected disability" (VA's sentence; the statute also reaches a Veteran who "died while a disability so evaluated was in existence" — a permanent and total service-connected rating at the time of death, whatever the cause — 38 U.S.C. 3501(a)(1)(A), (D); VA decides, row B lists the routes) → Chapter 35, or Fry for a qualifying death on or after 9/11/2001 (row B for both lists), VA Form 22-5490. VA: "Average time to process education claims: 30 days" (va.gov how to apply, 2026-08-17). The screens are signposts, not the full rule: answering "no" here does not mean "not eligible" — VA decides, and the eligibility pages linked in section B carry the routes these lines compress.

C · VR&E vs GI BILL
C · VR&E and the Post-9/11 GI Bill — the differences that decide things

VR&E · Chapter 31

Doorway: a service-connected rating of 10% or more lets you apply; "The VRC will determine if you're entitled" — and the standard depends on the rating. Rated 10%: VA must find a serious employment handicap — "For Veterans rated at 10 percent and Veterans beyond their 12-year basic period of eligibility, the finding of a SEH is necessary to establish entitlement to VR&E services." Rated 20% or more, within that period: "a finding of employment handicap results in entitlement to VR&E services." VA defines the serious one as "a significant impairment" of the ability "to prepare for, obtain, or retain employment consistent with his or her abilities, aptitudes, and interests" (VA VR&E program definitions, 2024-01-22). Not months of service.

Shape: a rehabilitation plan a VA counselor approves — the degree, the school, the timeline. The counselor is a named VA employee assigned to your case.

Pays for: tuition, fees, books, supplies, required equipment, licensing fees — with no in-state cap logic — plus a monthly subsistence allowance that does rise with dependents (about $195 a month from none to one on the FY2026 table, 2025-10-01 → 2026-09-30).

Monthly money: the standard table, or the Post-9/11 housing rate if you are also Chapter 33 eligible and elect it (VA Form 28-0987). Paid in bands — full, three-quarter and half time (exactly half time is paid). Less than half time: "is not payable," with two exceptions VA decides. 38 CFR 21.314

Post-9/11 GI Bill · Chapter 33

Doorway: months of qualifying service after 9/10/2001; the tier is fixed by service length, a Purple Heart, or a disability discharge.

Shape: you choose a school and a program VA has approved for GI Bill use (VA's Comparison Tool lists each school's "Approved programs of study"); the school certifies your enrollment in it; no VA counselor approves your plan.

Pays for: tuition and fees to the in-state public cap (Yellow Ribbon above it), a books stipend "up to $1,000 each academic year" (va.gov rates page, 2026-08-01 → 2027-07-31), and a housing allowance that is "not adjusted to include dependents."

Monthly money: the E-5-with-dependents housing rate for the school's ZIP, times your rate of pursuit rounded to the nearest 10%, times your tier — only above half time; half the national average if every class is online. 38 U.S.C. 3313(c)

ORDER OF USE — VA'S WORDS

"If you use VR&E benefits, we won't deduct entitlement from your other VA education benefits, like the Post-9/11 GI Bill or the Montgomery GI Bill." And the reverse: "If you've used benefits under any other VA education program and then you use VR&E benefits, we'll have to deduct from your remaining VR&E entitlement the amount of time used under the other VA education program." Two limits, kept apart. GI Bill side — VA: "Generally, if you're eligible for more than 1 education benefit, you may be able to get up to a maximum of 48 months (or 4 years) of VA education benefits." and "This doesn't include Veteran Readiness and Employment (VR&E) benefits (Chapter 31)." VR&E side — months used under another program are deducted from VR&E entitlement, and a retroactive induction can restore them: "we may retroactively approve your previous months of entitlement and return them to the other VA education program." va.gov VR&E eligibility, 2025-11-07 · VR&E SCO Handbook § G.5 · va.gov eligibility · 38 U.S.C. 3695

SWITCHING

You cannot draw both for the same period. One choice is permanent: VA: "If you have only 1 period of service that begins on or after August 1, 2011, and you qualify for other VA education benefits, you can use only 1 education benefit." "If you choose to use the Post-9/11 GI Bill instead of the Montgomery GI Bill Active Duty (MGIB-AD, or Chapter 30), you can't switch at some later date to use MGIB-AD" — and the reverse (va.gov education eligibility, 2026-04-03). Otherwise, between GI Bill chapters a switch happens "at the start of your next enrollment period"; into Chapter 31, VA's handbook for schools: "A change in the election of VA benefits in the middle of a semester or term is not allowed." Practical advice, not a VA rule: tell your counselor and the certifying office before the term starts. va.gov change benefits · VR&E SCO Handbook § G.3, 2025-07-30

Not sure you have a rating? A rating comes from a disability claim. An accredited Veterans Service Officer files it with you for free. That is a different office from this one, and a different benefit from any on this page — but it is the doorway to VR&E.
D · TWO RULES
D · The two rules that move the housing money on Chapter 33 — and why Chapter 31 is different
1 · MORE THAN HALF TIME (CHAPTER 33 ONLY)

Chapter 33 housing allowance: "To be eligible for MHA, your rate of pursuit must be more than 50%." Rate of pursuit is your credits divided by what the school calls full time for that term — and at ODU the divisor changes in summer. Undergraduate: "12 or more credit hours during the fall and spring semesters" is full time; "During the summer term, an undergraduate student is considered to be full time if he or she is enrolled in nine hours" (ODU undergraduate catalog, 2026–27). Graduate: "A minimum of nine credit hours is considered to be a full-time course load for graduate students per fall and spring semesters"; "In summer semesters, six (6) credit hours constitute a full-time load" (ODU graduate policies, 2026–27) — both read 2026-09-02; ODU republishes each academic year. Sessions shorter than the standard term (eight-week, six-week, Maymester, winter) are converted by VA to equivalent credits before the division — the planner does that arithmetic; the SCO certifies which full-time standard applies to your term, so ask before you plan on a divisor. Exactly half is not more than half — on Chapter 33. va.gov rates page · 38 CFR 21.9505

Chapter 31 does not use this rule. Its subsistence follows training time in bands — full, three-quarter, half — and exactly half time is paid: the FY2026 table has a half-time row ("½ Time" $408.66 / $506.32 / $595.16 / +$44.42, 2025-10-01 → 2026-09-30, VA FY2026 Chapter 31 rates), and the elected Post-9/11 rate pays the entire, three-quarter or half ZIP figure in residence, or half, three-eighths or one-quarter of the national average when solely online (38 CFR 21.260(c)). Only less than half time is unpaid — subsistence "is not payable" below half time, with two exceptions VA decides (38 CFR 21.314). Which band you are in depends on the full-time standard the school certifies (38 CFR 21.4270(c)) — the planner shows it stretch by stretch.

2 · ONE CLASS NOT ENTIRELY ONLINE

"If you take at least 1 in-person course, we'll pay the standard housing allowance." A term that is entirely distance learning pays half the national average instead — up to $1,261 (2026-08-01 → 2027-07-31) rather than the ZIP rate. VA treats a hybrid class, or an on-campus proctored exam, as in residence — "SCOs will certify all courses which combine Classroom Instruction and Distance Learning (Hybrid) as In-Residence training" (VA Education Service, Hybrid Training Updates, 2019); the School Certifying Official certifies which column a class is in. va.gov online learning, 2025-06-11 · 38 CFR 21.9640(b)(1)(ii)
