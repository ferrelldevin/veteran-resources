WHO

Who is this — which benefit, which lane

1

"Which benefit am I on?" — or you can't tell from what they're saying

Ask to see their Certificate of Eligibility (COE) or award letter, or ask: "Do you have a VA counselor — Veteran Readiness and Employment?"
VR&E
Chapter 31. Their VA counselor (VRC) sends the school a training authorization; the student does not submit the clearance form. Route anything about their plan, books, or monthly money to the counselor; anything about the school's certification to the SCO. Hand them Which Benefit Am I On if they want the difference explained.
GI BILL
Chapter 33, 30, 1606, 35, or Fry. ODU's clearance-form process covers Chapters 1606, 30, 33 and 35 (ODU's FAQ), with every-semester exceptions for active-duty, MEM Ashore and certificate students; the SCO certifies. Go to row 3 for the exact rule.
UNSURE
Post-9/11 students can check their own status at va.gov/education/check-remaining-benefits. For every other chapter the COE letter or the VA Education Call Center answers it (888-442-4551, TTY 711 — va.gov payments FAQ); the SCO can also look. Do not guess the chapter — the pay rules differ.
the student can name their chapter, and you know which lane they are in.
ODU veteran student checklist · va.gov letters tool (Chapter 33 only, 2024-11-29)
2

"Do I qualify for VR&E?" — or "what's the difference from my GI Bill?"

First: are they a veteran (discharged), or still serving on active duty? Then the criterion for that lane — veteran: do they have a VA service-connected disability rating of 10% or more? Active duty: do they have a 20%-or-higher pre-discharge rating and a coming separation, or are they awaiting discharge for a severe illness or injury?
VETERAN, RATED 10%+
They may apply — that is eligibility to apply, not a decision. VA: "You have a service-connected disability rating of at least 10% from VA" plus a discharge other than dishonorable; then "The VRC will determine if you're entitled to receive VR&E benefits and services." What the VRC decides, in VA's definitions: at 10%, "the finding of a SEH is necessary to establish entitlement to VR&E services" — a serious employment handicap; at 20% or more within the 12-year basic period, "a finding of employment handicap results in entitlement to VR&E services" (VA VR&E program definitions, 2024-01-22). The form is VA Form 28-1900 on va.gov. Discharged on or after January 1, 2013: "There's no time limit on your eligibility." What it can pay for, in the statute's words: "tuition, fees, books, supplies, handling charges, licensing fees, and equipment," plus a monthly subsistence allowance and counseling. Order of use, in VA's words: "If you use VR&E benefits, we won't deduct entitlement from your other VA education benefits"; the reverse deducts from VR&E — "But if you already used other VA education benefits and we determine that you're eligible for VR&E, we may retroactively approve your previous months of entitlement and return them to the other VA education program." Route the conversation to the Military Connection Center or a VRC.
ACTIVE DUTY
A different doorway — not the 10% rating. VA's two active-duty routes: "You have a 20% or higher pre-discharge disability rating and will soon leave the military, or you're waiting to be discharged because of a severe illness or injury." Either one: they may apply, and the VRC decides — on the form VA names for their status: with a VA disability rating, "Fill out an Application for Veteran Readiness and Employment for Claimants with Service-Connected Disabilities (VA Form 28-1900)"; a service member "who hasn't yet received a service-connected disability rating" — in the Physical Evaluation Board process, "expecting an other than dishonorable discharge and have a VA memorandum rating of 20% or more," or "entered in the Integrated Disability Evaluation System (IDES)" — "Instead, fill out VA Vocational Rehabilitation - Getting Ahead After You Get Out (VA Form 28-0588)" (va.gov, how to apply for VR&E, updated 2026-04-09). Without a rating, the doorway is conditional, not open: VA — "You don't need to wait for a rating" — then "At least 1 of these must be true: You're going through the Physical Evaluation Board process, or You're expecting an other than dishonorable discharge and have a VA memorandum rating of 20% or more, or You're entered in the Integrated Disability Evaluation System (IDES)"; and separately, "Severely injured active-duty service members can automatically receive VR&E benefits before VA issues a disability rating" (same va.gov page). None of those established, or the student is unsure which applies → hand the facts to a VRC; do not tell them they can apply. The desk never decides that a service member must wait for discharge, and never decides that they may apply without a rating — the VRC does, on the facts; a VSO can help with the disability claim in parallel. Note for the money question: a service member on active duty in Chapter 31 training is not paid subsistence (VR&E SCO Handbook § F.3, on the planner).
VETERAN, NO RATING
Without a rating of 10% or more, the veteran route is not open yet. A rating comes from a disability claim, and VA says "the services an accredited VSO representative provides on your VA benefit claims are always free" — that is a VSO's lane, not this office's. Hand them Which Benefit Am I On, which names who does what.
the student knows which of three they are — veteran with a rating of 10% or more (apply, VA Form 28-1900), active duty on one of VA's two routes (apply — 28-1900 with a VA rating; 28-0588 without one only on PEB, the 20% memorandum rating or IDES; none or unsure → the VRC), or veteran without a rating yet (VSO claim first) — and leaves with the form name and the role to ask, not an opinion on whether they'll be approved.

Getting paid

3

"I registered. What do I do to get certified and paid?"

Chapter 31, or a GI Bill chapter? (Row 1 if unsure.) For a GI Bill chapter: is the student active-duty military, MEM Ashore, or in a certificate program — and is this their first term here, or has something changed (major, chapter, duty status, a break, a term they asked not to be certified)?
CH 31
Nothing to submit here — the counselor's authorization comes to the school. Ask the SCO whether it has arrived; if not, the student contacts their counselor. Go to row 11 if an authorization email is what you're holding.
GI BILL
Every semester for three groups, ODU's FAQ: "Active duty military, MEM Ashore, and certificate students using GI Bill® benefits must complete the Veterans Clearance Form EACH semester AFTER registering for ODU classes." Everyone else: new here, or something changed? Submit the VCF with the COE uploaded, after registering. Continuing with no changes, and not in those three groups? Nothing to submit — ODU's sentence below. ODU's FAQ names the chapters: "Students using VA Educational Benefits (Chapter 1606 (Reserve) – Montgomery GI Bill®, Chapter 30 (Active Duty) – Montgomery GI Bill®, Chapter 33 – Post 9/11 GI Bill®, or Chapter 35 – Survivors and Dependents Educational Assistance) need to submit a Veteran Clearance Form with their Certificate of Eligibility from the VA attached to it." ODU's two pages disagree on Chapter 35: the checklist says "Students using VA Education Benefits (Chapter 1606, 30, or 33) will need to submit a Veterans Clearance Form (VCF)" and does not mention Chapter 35 — so a Chapter 35 student asks the SCO whether the form is required here; the SCO's instruction governs, not this sheet's reading of two ODU pages. ODU: certification starts "approximately 30 days before classes begin" and "allow at least 30-45 business days for initial certification processing." ODU's FAQ: "If you have not made any changes and were certified for VA Benefits in the prior semester, you DO NOT need to submit a Veterans Clearance Form. You will be certified automatically and receive a confirmation email"; a new one is required "when there is a change in major, duty status (Active Duty/Veteran), selected Chapter benefit, a break in enrollment, or after a student has requested not to be certified for a term". Submit it "at least 30 days before the start of the semester". Chapter 31: "Chapter 31 students DO NOT need to complete the Veterans Clearance Form. Chapter 31 students will coordinate with their assigned VA Educational Counselor."
one of three, and the student knows which: VCF submitted (first term here, after a change, or one of ODU's three every-semester groups) · automatic-certification route confirmed (continuing GI Bill student, no changes, not in those groups — nothing to submit; ODU's confirmation email is the evidence) · Chapter 31 authorization confirmed in hand by the SCO (nothing for the student to submit).

4

"I haven't been paid."

In this order — stop at the first step that fails, that step is the branch. 1. To the SCO: "Has this student's enrollment been certified for this term?" No → NOT CERTIFIED. 2. To the SCO: "Does the certification match what they are actually taking — credits, dates, in-person or online?" No → CERTIFIED, WRONG. 3. To the student: which chapter? Since January 2026 VA requires monthly verification of every education beneficiary — Post-9/11 (including Fry and the Edith Nourse Rogers STEM Scholarship), Montgomery GI Bill (Active Duty and Selected Reserve), Chapter 35, VET TEC 2.0 — so for any of those, ask 4. "Have you verified this month?" No → VERIFY DUE. Chapter 31 is not on VA's verification list; a Chapter 31 payment question goes to the counselor. All four clear → CERTIFIED OK.
NOT CERTIFIED
Step 1 failed — that is the first blocker and it is school-side. Usual causes: no VCF submitted (row 3), or for Chapter 31 no authorization received (row 11). Tell the student plainly: "Our office hasn't certified yet — here is what's missing," and fix the missing piece today.
CERTIFIED, WRONG
The certification does not match the student's real schedule — wrong credits, dates, or in-person/online split — so the money is wrong too. The SCO amends it in Enrollment Manager; VA's own guidance to students whose enrollment information is incorrect is to ask the SCO to update it. Nothing else moves until this does.
VERIFY DUE
Certified correctly, and the student has not verified this month. VA: "As of January 2026, all education beneficiaries, including Survivors' and Dependents' Educational Assistance (Chapter 35) students, must verify their enrollment each month to receive benefit payments" (benefits.va.gov/gibill, updated 2026-08-06). VA's verification page lists who can verify online: "Post-9/11 GI Bill, Montgomery GI Bill Active Duty (MGIB-AD), Montgomery GI Bill Selected Reserve (MGIB-SR), Veteran Employment Through Technology Education Courses 2.0 (VET TEC 2.0), Survivors' and Dependents' Educational Assistance (DEA)" — online, by text, by email, through Ask VA, or by phone; STEM Scholarship students verify differently: VA — "If you're using the STEM Scholarship, you can't verify your enrollment by email" and "If you don't want to verify your enrollment online, or if you're using the Science Technology Engineering Math (STEM) Scholarship, you can verify a different way" — so a STEM student who says "I answered the email" has not verified; send them to the page's other methods (va.gov, Verify your school enrollment, updated 2026-07-27). Chapter 31 is not on that list — the counselor answers a Chapter 31 payment question. VA's older FAQ (2025-12-22, before the January 2026 change) still carries the consequence sentences: "We'll pause your monthly benefit payments after 2 consecutive months of not verifying your enrollment" (Post-9/11) and "We won't send your monthly benefits payment if you don't verify your enrollment" (Montgomery); treat its narrower who-must-verify wording as superseded. What follows is VA's: for Montgomery, "We process your benefit payment after you verify your enrollment. It can take up to 5 days to arrive"; a paused Post-9/11 payment is a VA status question (row 4, last branch).
CERTIFIED OK
Certified correctly, verification done or not required — the school's part is finished, and the rest is VA's to answer, not the desk's to diagnose. VA's own timing, and it differs by payment — a first payment: "we'll deposit your payment into your bank account 7 to 10 business days after you verify your school enrollment"; by check, "about 14 days after you verify" (va.gov payments FAQ, updated 2026-08-05); a monthly Post-9/11 housing payment: "We process your benefit payment on the first day of every month. It can take up to 5 days to arrive"; housing allowance is paid "at the end of each month" for the month just finished, so the first payment of a term normally arrives the month after classes start; Chapters 30, 1606 and 35: the monthly payment follows that month's verification ("We process your benefit payment after you verify your enrollment. It can take up to 5 days to arrive"). If the clock that applies has run out with nothing received, the student asks VA — award processed? payment suspended? direct-deposit details right? — those are questions only VA can answer. (ODU's "30-45 business days" is the school's certification window, not VA's payment clock; once certified, it no longer applies.) Who the money goes to depends on the chapter: Chapters 33 and 31 pay tuition to the school and the housing or subsistence allowance — when the student is eligible for one (row 5 has VA's exclusions: active duty, a spouse on transferred benefits while the Veteran is on active duty, half time or less, correspondence or flight training, school breaks) — to the student; Chapters 30, 1606 and 35 pay the monthly amount to the student, who pays the school. Status: the VA Education Call Center (888-442-4551, TTY 711 — va.gov payments FAQ) or ask.va.gov in writing. Chapter 31 students ask their counselor whether the award is processed.
the student knows which of four things is true — not certified, certified wrong, verification due, or the school's part is done and the question is VA's — and what their next ask is.

"Am I full time?" — "How much will I get?" — "Will I get the full housing rate?"

Chapter 33, Chapter 31, or another chapter? (Row 1 if unsure.) For Chapter 33, one more question before any housing talk: is the student on active duty, or a spouse using transferred benefits while the Veteran is on active duty? If yes → no housing allowance at all, whatever the schedule (VA's list, CH 33 branch).
CH 33
First VA's exclusions, then the two conditions. VA: "You're not eligible for an MHA payment if any of these are true:" "You're on active duty"; "You're a spouse using transferred benefits while the Veteran is on active duty"; "You're in school half time or less than half time"; "You're taking correspondence training or flight training"; "You're on break from school" (va.gov rates page, 2026-07-31). Any of those → no housing figure, tuition and fees still paid; the planner asks the active-duty question and returns UNKNOWN or none. Otherwise hand them the Semester Pay Planner (address in the footer) and say the two conditions — they decide whether housing is paid and on which basis, not the amount: (1) more than half time — VA: "your rate of pursuit must be more than 50%" for any housing allowance; (2) at least one class not entirely online to get the ZIP-code rate, otherwise half the national average. The amount is then that basis × the rate of pursuit rounded to the nearest 10% × the student's eligibility tier — only a full-time student at the 100% tier gets the full ZIP figure. Full time at ODU is per term: 12 undergraduate / 9 graduate in fall and spring, 9 / 6 in summer; the SCO certifies the standard. The planner shows the figure for each stretch of the term with the rule beside it.
CH 31
Different rule — training time by band, and the band depends on the full-time standard the school certifies to VA — do not name a band at the desk. VA's table: at a 14-hour standard, 14 is full time, 10–13 three-quarter, 7–9 half; "When 12 hours is properly certified as full time, VA will measure 9 through 11 hours as 3/4 time, 6 through 8 hours as 1/2 time" (38 CFR 21.4270(c) and its footnote). ODU's catalog calls 12 full time for undergraduates and 9 for graduate students, but which standard ODU certifies to VA is the SCO's answer, not the catalog's. Send the student to the Semester Pay Planner with the standard the SCO gives them; students who elected the Post-9/11 rate follow the in-person rule too (entire, ¾ or ½ of the ZIP figure). The counselor's plan governs.
OTHER
Chapters 30, 1606 and 35 pay a monthly rate by training-time band (full, ¾, ½), set by what the SCO certifies. Below half time the three chapters differ: Chapters 30 and 35 pay a rate capped at tuition and fees, possibly as one lump sum; Chapter 1606's table has a plain below-half row — VA: "Less than 1/2-time enrollment: $123.25 for each full month" (FY2026) — with no tuition cap (row 7, OTHER). The rates and their dates are on Which Benefit Am I On. The band itself is the SCO's answer, not yours.
the active-duty / transferred-benefits question was asked first; then the hand-over is made and the student has heard "more than half time" and "one class in person" in those words — or, if an exclusion applies, that there is no housing allowance to plan on.

6

"Does this class count as in person?" (hybrid, online with an on-campus exam, live video)

First: is this remedial, deficiency, or developmental coursework (below college level — a course the school requires before the degree-level one)? If yes, stop at the REMEDIAL branch; the hybrid rule below does not apply. Then, for every other class: does the class require the student on campus at any point during the term — class meetings, an in-person midterm or final, an exam proctored on campus by university staff?
REMEDIAL
Straight to the SCO — the hybrid rule does not reach these classes. VA to certifying officials: "Per CFR 21.4267(g) VA does not approve any Remedial/Deficiency courses that are not taught 100% resident in classroom. Since they cannot be approved unless offered as 100% in residence the changes regarding hybrid courses do not apply to Remedial/Deficiency courses." Asked directly about a remedial course "that has online instruction but in-person testing requirements on our campus": "No." So a remedial class taught fully in a classroom is in residence; a remedial class with any online instruction is not certifiable as hybrid — whether it can be certified at all is the SCO's question, not the desk's. The regulation itself: "Remedial and deficiency courses offered by independent study cannot be approved" (38 CFR 21.4267(g)). Write down the course number and send it.
YES
For a non-remedial class, VA certifies it as in residence. VA to certifying officials: "SCOs will certify all courses which combine Classroom Instruction and Distance Learning (Hybrid) as In-Residence training" — because they are "not offered solely through distance learning." A live-video class counts only if the students watch it together on campus.
NO
Distance learning. Watching live from home is distance. A single in-person meeting before the term starts does not count. One distance class among in-person ones changes nothing; a term that is entirely distance pays the online rate.
UNSURE
The SCO decides how the class is certified — not the desk, not the student. Write down the class and send the question to the SCO. VA is also rewriting these definitions (RIN 2900-AQ89, not final as of 2026-09-02); if that rule has issued, the SCO knows first.
the remedial question was asked first; then the class is either clearly in one column or written down for the SCO.

7

Student is at half time or below and asks about their monthly money

Which chapter — and for Chapter 31, does the student say VA has approved reduced work tolerance or an extended evaluation plan?
CH 33
No housing allowance at 50% or below. The statute pays it only "on more than a half-time basis." Tuition and fees are still certified and paid to the school at their tier. Exactly half time is not more than half.
CH 31 +
Chapter 31 with a VA determination on file (reduced work tolerance, or quarter-time under an Individualized Extended Evaluation Plan): VA pays per the counselor's plan. Route to the counselor; certification proceeds as normal.
CH 31
Chapter 31 with no such determination: below half time the allowance "is not payable." The two exceptions are VA's decisions — a VA physician decides reduced work tolerance on the counselor's referral — so route the student to their counselor rather than answering yes or no yourself. Enrollment still gets certified either way.
OTHER
Chapters 30, 1606 and 35 pay a monthly rate by training-time band at half time and above — and below half time the three chapters differ. Chapters 30 and 35: VA's tables read "Less than 1/2-time and more than 1/4-time enrollment" and "1/4-time enrollment or less", each "for each full month (or the cost of your tuition and fees, whichever is less)", and "If your tuition and fees are less than the monthly rate, we'll send you a lump sum (1 payment) at the start of each term" (va.gov MGIB-AD (Chapter 30) rates, updated 2026-06-23; va.gov DEA (Chapter 35) rates, updated 2026-03-25). Chapter 1606: VA's MGIB-SR table has one below-half row, "Less than 1/2-time enrollment: $123.25 for each full month" — a monthly rate with no tuition cap, effective 2025-10-01 to 2026-09-30 (va.gov MGIB-SR (Chapter 1606) rates, updated 2025-11-17). So below half time the desk promises no figure of its own: for Chapters 30 and 35 the amount is capped by tuition and may arrive as one payment; for Chapter 1606 it is the table's row. The SCO certifies the band; the rates by band are on Which Benefit Am I On. Fry Scholarship students follow the Chapter 33 rule above.
the student knows the half-time rule for their chapter and who decides exceptions.

CHANGES

Classes and money — changing a schedule

8

"I want to drop a class" — or "I'm going to withdraw"

Three things before any answer. 1. Which chapter? (Row 1.) Chapter 31 → the counselor decides changes to an authorized plan; tell the SCO and the student calls the VRC. 2. Has the SCO already certified this class to VA, and has VA already paid on it (tuition to the school; housing, books or a monthly amount to the student)? If yes to either, a drop of any kind is an SCO debt-review question — VA lists "Student never attended classes certified" and "Student withdrew on or before the first day of term" among the causes of a school debt, and "School debts result from overpayments of tuition, fees, and Yellow Ribbon. Student debts result from housing, books & supplies, and allowances under programs that provide them" (VA, Information About GI Bill Overpayments and Debts, updated 2026-05-18). 3. Has the class (or the term) started? Not yet → NOT STARTED. Started → is today inside the add/drop period for that class's session? ODU Fall 2026 full term: deadline Sep 2, 2026. Spring 2027 full term: deadline Jan 20, 2027. If the deadline has passed, you are on the NO branch. 8-week sessions and ODUGlobal have their own dates on the calendar.
NOT STARTED
Dropped before its first day: never attended — but not "nothing follows". If the SCO had not yet certified it, the SCO simply leaves it off. If it was certified, the SCO amends the certification, and if VA had already paid on it the SCO reviews for a debt: VA names "Student withdrew on or before the first day of term" as a school-debt cause, and for the student, books-and-supplies or housing already paid on that class can be an overpayment ("In some of these instances, you may create a debt to the VA if you're overpaid for tuition, books, or housing" — VA, Understanding Your Award Letter, updated 2026-02-20). Tell the SCO the same day.
YES
Inside add/drop, after the class has started: a reduction from the drop date — not "never enrolled". (The quoted VA answers below are Chapter 33 scenarios; for Chapters 30, 1606 and 35 the monthly amount was paid to the student and any overpayment is the student's; for Chapter 31 the counselor and the authorization govern — the SCO says which applies.) VA to certifying officials: "The charges for the original credit load must be reported (using the 'Amend' function) even if the student reduces during the drop/add period. SCOs will follow with an adjusted certification that includes the reduced credits and corresponding charges" — "The law entitles the student to a prorated T&Fs payment" (VA Education Service, December 2021 office hours, slide 20). And: "If this reduction happened during drop/add, the 6-credit hour does not apply. This student would be entitled to a prorated payment- part of the term at 12 credits, part at 9"; "A drop that occurs after the 1st day of the term doesn't create any books and supplies debt, but there may be housing debt, dependent on whether VA makes the reduction in credit hours before the affected housing payment is released" (VA Education Service, August 2021 office hours, slide 35). So: no W, no mitigating-circumstances question, no books debt — but the housing rate is recomputed from the drop date, and housing already paid on the old number can come back as a debt. Tell the SCO the day it happens, before VA pays on the old number. The SCO determines how it is reported; the student should not plan money on a drop until the SCO has. Row 5 for what the new credit count does to the rate.
NO
Stop — send them to the SCO before they drop, not after. A withdrawal with a non-punitive grade (a W) after the drop period sends benefits back to "the first day of the term" unless there are mitigating circumstances, and money already paid becomes a debt. VA "also automatically grants mitigating circumstances for up to 6 credits the first time, and only the first time, you reduce or terminate and ask for mitigating circumstances" — the ask is part of the rule; nothing is automatic until the student asks. VA's route for asking: "the easiest way is to tell your School Certifying Official (SCO)… they can also send us your mitigating circumstances"; otherwise VA writes to the student asking for them, and the student must "write the reasons and mail them to your Regional Processing Office or send them using the 'Submit a Question' button" (VA, Changes or Withdrawal of Classes, 2021). So the desk's terminal here is: the student tells the SCO the reasons for the drop, in writing, when they drop. A failing grade is punitive and does not trigger this. ODU's last day to withdraw: Nov 13, 2026 (Fall full term), Apr 9, 2027 (Spring).
the SCO has been consulted before the drop — knowing the chapter, whether the class was already certified or paid on, whether it had started, and the drop date — and, for a drop after add/drop, the student has given the SCO the reasons so mitigating circumstances can be asked for; or the student has chosen to drop knowing the rule.

9

"Can I switch from my GI Bill to VR&E?" (or the other way, or they mix the two up)

Is the term already under way?
YES
Not in the middle of the term. Into Chapter 31, VA's handbook for schools: "A change in the election of VA benefits in the middle of a semester or term is not allowed" — "the certification for the CH31 cannot begin until the start of the new semester or term." Between GI Bill chapters, VA: "Switch to using a different education benefit at the start of your next enrollment period" — and which period counts as "next" is read off the student's actual certifications by the SCO (a separately certified 8-week session may be one; this sheet does not decide that). One switch is permanent: VA: "If you have only 1 period of service that begins on or after August 1, 2011, and you qualify for other VA education benefits, you can use only 1 education benefit" — "If you choose to use the Post-9/11 GI Bill instead of the Montgomery GI Bill Active Duty (MGIB-AD, or Chapter 30), you can't switch at some later date to use MGIB-AD" and the reverse. That choice is the student's and a counselor's, never the desk's. Either way the switch is set up by the counselor (Chapter 31) and the SCO, not the desk.
NO
Between terms it is a conversation with a VRC (for VR&E) and the SCO (for how certification changes). Say these in VA's words: "If you use VR&E benefits, we won't deduct entitlement from your other VA education benefits"; the reverse deducts from VR&E unless VA grants a retroactive induction. Two different limits, do not merge them: the GI Bill side — VA: "Generally, if you're eligible for more than 1 education benefit, you may be able to get up to a maximum of 48 months (or 4 years) of VA education benefits." and "This doesn't include Veteran Readiness and Employment (VR&E) benefits (Chapter 31)." — and the VR&E side, where months used under another program are deducted from VR&E entitlement unless VA grants a retroactive induction. The counselor explains the student's own months.
the student knows which program they are on today and who owns the switch.

10

"How much benefit do I have left?"

Which benefit were they awarded — a GI Bill chapter (30, 33, 1606, Fry), DEA (Chapter 35), transferred Post-9/11, or Chapter 31?
GI BILL · DEA · TRANSFERRED
Self-service for anyone VA has awarded: va.gov/education/check-remaining-benefits — "If we awarded you VA education benefits, you can check online to find out how much of your benefits you've used and how much you have remaining." The page routes by form, not by chapter: "If you're already getting GI Bill benefits — You'll need to use VA Form 22-1990"; "If you're already getting Survivors' and Dependents' Educational Assistance (DEA) benefits — You'll need to use VA Form 22-5490"; "If you're already getting transferred Post-9/11 GI Bill benefits — You'll need to use VA Form 22-1990e" (va.gov, check remaining benefits, updated 2026-08-17). Sign-in is the student's, on their own device — never on the desk's. The Post-9/11 decision letter is downloadable from the letters tool (Post-9/11 only, for now).
TOOL WON'T SHOW IT · UNDECIDED · DISPUTED
The tool needs the student's own sign-in and a decided claim. If it will not sign in or shows no statement, hand the question to the SCO — Enrollment Manager shows it. VA to certifying officials: "The remaining entitlement for a student's current benefit type displays in months and days on the student's Profile and Benefits tab" (VA Education Office Hours, Enrollment Manager quick tips (updated 2025-06-04); the 2026-03-07 update moved it out of the summary box — "no longer displays the remaining Chapter 33 entitlement" in the BLUF box, Enrollment Manager system updates, 2026-03-07). The SCO reads it, not the desk. A figure the student disputes, or a claim still being decided, is VA's question: the VA Education Call Center (888-442-4551, TTY 711 — va.gov payments FAQ) or ask.va.gov in writing, naming the school and term.
CH 31
Chapter 31 is not on VA's check-remaining page (its three forms are GI Bill, DEA and transferred Post-9/11). Months of VR&E entitlement are the counselor's: route the student to their VRC. The SCO can still read the current benefit's remaining entitlement in Enrollment Manager (Profile and Benefits tab — VA sentence in the branch above), and the COE or award letter they were mailed states it. Note for the others: the online letters tool is Post-9/11 only ("Right now, you can use this tool only to get your decision letter for Post-9/11 GI Bill benefits") — that limit is the letters tool's, not the remaining-benefits check's.
the student has VA's online check by the form that matches their benefit, or the SCO has read Enrollment Manager for them, or — for a disputed figure or an undecided claim — VA's call center or written route, or — Chapter 31 — their counselor; never a figure from you.

INBOX

Paperwork in the inbox

11

An email from VA about a Chapter 31 "training authorization" is in the inbox — today's, or one you just found from last month

Are you the designated SCO?
NO
Forward it to the SCO now and confirm, out loud or in writing, that they saw it. Plan as if no reminder is coming. A student's monthly money waits on this. If the email is old, say when it arrived — an authorization that sat a term can mean a student went unpaid a term. Whether the counselor named on it is contacted is the SCO's call: the handbook's sentence is that the SCO "may contact the case manager" with questions about the authorization, not a duty to explain the delay.
YES
The authorization permits training; it is not proof of enrollment. The SCO certifies the student's actual enrollment, matched against the authorization — never the email itself. First the checks VA puts before certifying: is the student registered, and are the courses required for the approved program the authorization names — VA's first-year scenario: "Verify John's courses are required for his chosen approved program", and only then "Certify John's enrollment through Enrollment Manager, providing required details such as credit or clock hours, and tuition and fees" (VA, First-Year SCO Scenarios, updated 2026-06-10). What is certified, in VA's words: "an official report you submit to VA through Enrollment Manager. It confirms key enrollment details for each student" — program, term dates, in residence or distance, hours, tuition and fees — gathered with "supporting enrollment or training documentation" (VA, Certification Basics for new SCOs, updated 2026-06-02). From the handbook: use "the same facility code or sub-facility code as indicated in the authorization", certify "prior to the drop-add period", and identify "if training is in residence or distance learning hours". Not registered, or the program, campus, dates or courses do not match → row 12. The statute says schools report enrollment "without delay."
the SCO has confirmed they have it — not "I forwarded it" — and what gets certified is the student's enrollment, checked against the authorization, not the authorization.

12

A Chapter 31 authorization does not match (wrong program, wrong campus, wrong dates, wrong facility code)

Is the discrepancy written down and in the SCO's hands? The desk does not decide that an authorization is "rejected" — that word is not the desk's, and this sheet states no rule about what the school may do with an authorization it disagrees with; the handbook's instruction is to ask the case manager.
NO
Write down exactly what does not match (program, campus, dates, facility code, amounts) and hand the authorization to the SCO. The SCO contacts the case manager named on it — VA's handbook for schools: "If you have any questions regarding the authorization, you may contact the case manager listed in the 'VR&E Case Manager Email' section of the authorization." Whether anything is certified or invoiced in the meantime is the SCO's decision with the case manager, on the authorization as clarified or corrected — not a rule this desk applies.
YES
Nothing more from the desk. The SCO decides on the clarified or corrected authorization and keeps the case-manager contact on file. Tell the student the school has asked the counselor's office; the timing is VA's, and not something the desk can promise. If the student's classes are meanwhile at risk, that is the SCO's call to escalate, and the student can call their VRC (row 2).
the discrepancy is written down, the SCO has it, the SCO's contact with the case manager is documented, and the SCO has decided on the clarified or corrected authorization.

13

Someone asks you to certify, or to enter something in Enrollment Manager

Are you a designated SCO who has completed VA's required training?
NO
Then you do not certify — but you may be allowed to enter. VA: "SCO Assistants… can enter data into the system but cannot submit enrollments to VA." Work-study staff "cannot certify information to VA." If an existing SCO has granted you Assistant access to Enrollment Manager, you may enter the data; submitting it to VA — certifying — is the designated SCO's act, every time. Without Assistant access, gather the paperwork and hand it to the SCO. VA's training is optional for you, not a licence.
YES
Then you certify the student's enrollment — not the paper someone handed you. Verify the actual enrollment details from the registration record — program, term dates, in residence or distance, hours, the applicable tuition and fees — and keep "supporting enrollment or training documentation" for every certification (VA, Certification Basics for new SCOs, updated 2026-06-02). A Chapter 31 authorization is permission, not proof of enrollment: row 11. A request to certify something you cannot verify from the record is a no, whoever asks.
not the SCO: the request is in a designated SCO's hands and anything you entered was under Assistant access an SCO granted — you submitted nothing. The SCO: what was certified is the verified enrollment, with its supporting documentation kept.

Someone wants to call Tungsten or the "Financial Services Center" about a student's payment

Is the question about a student's monthly money, or about the school's tuition invoice?
STUDENT
Stop the call — wrong pipeline. Tungsten Network is VA's vendor e-invoicing channel — the handbook for schools: "The Tungsten Network is an electronic invoicing service that the VA uses to authorize and pay all invoices"; invoices go "directly to VA FSC ECB… for processing into the VA FSC payment system" — the system that pays vendors, which is what the school is when it bills tuition. It is not where a student's housing or subsistence payment lives. Use row 4 instead: SCO for certification, the VA Education Call Center or the counselor for the payment.
INVOICE
School invoice questions are the SCO's or the business office's lane — route there.
nobody spent a day on the invoice line for a student payment.
